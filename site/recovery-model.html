The Joint Replacement Recovery Model

The JRRZ trajectory is a conceptual educational model. It shows why surgery can create a short-term loss of function before rehabilitation, healing, and adaptation support longer-term improvement.

How to read the graph

The vertical direction represents patient-reported function. The horizontal direction represents time. The shaded range communicates that recovery differs among people; the line is not a promise, clinical prediction, or substitute for individualized assessment.

Recovery phases

  1. Decision and preparation: understanding the procedure, planning support, and setting realistic expectations.
  2. Surgery and immediate recovery: a controlled intervention followed by a temporary capability dip.
  3. Early recovery: protection, healing, safe movement, and initial rehabilitation.
  4. Functional recovery: rebuilding practical movement, daily activities, and confidence.
  5. Strength and stability: improving endurance, coordination, and sustainable function.
  6. Long-term joint health: maintaining mobility and responding promptly to meaningful changes.
