Patient Recovery Roadmap

This roadmap describes common phases of the joint-replacement journey without promising a universal timeline. Individual instructions from the treating team always take priority.

Remember: recovery is rarely a perfectly straight line. Progress, rest, setbacks, adaptation, and rebuilding can all be part of the process.

1. Decision and preparation

  • Understand the proposed procedure and alternatives.
  • Prepare the home, transportation, support, and mobility equipment.
  • Ask the care team what to expect before, during, and after surgery.

2. Surgery and immediate recovery

  • Expect a temporary reduction in function after the intervention.
  • Prioritize safety, pain-control instructions, wound care, and basic movement.
  • Know which warning signs require urgent contact or emergency care.

3. Early recovery

  • Begin movement and rehabilitation according to individualized instructions.
  • Use prescribed assistive devices and protect against falls.
  • Track useful functions rather than comparing recovery with another patient.

4. Functional recovery

  • Improve walking, transfers, stairs, balance, and daily activities.
  • Build consistency with prescribed exercises and pacing.
  • Discuss plateaus or unexpected changes with the care team.

5. Strength and stability

  • Rebuild supporting muscles, endurance, coordination, and confidence.
  • Increase activity gradually within professional guidance.
  • Continue focusing on movement quality and safe function.

6. Long-term joint health

  • Maintain sustainable strength and mobility habits.
  • Attend recommended follow-up and raise new concerns early.
  • Protect the implant and surrounding body systems through informed activity choices.
